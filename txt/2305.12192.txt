Volatility jumps and the classication of monetary policy announcements Giampiero M. Gallo ∗ Demetrio Lacava† Edoardo Otranto ‡ Abstract Central Banks interventions are frequent in response to exogenous events with direct implications on nancial market volatility. In this paper, we introduce the Asymmetric Jump Multiplicative Error Model (AJM), which accounts for a specic jump component of volatility within an intradaily framework. Taking the Federal Reserve (Fed) as a reference, we propose a new model–based classication of monetary announcements based on their impact on the jump component of volatility. Focusing on a short window following each Fed’s communi- cation, we isolate the impact of monetary announcements from any contamination carried by relevant events that may occur within the same announcement day. Keywords: Financial markets, Realized volatility, Signicant jumps, Monetary policy an- nouncements, Multiplicative Error Model. Either way, the announcement has an impact on market activity, with increases in the number of trades that, in turn, may translate into price changes and jumps in volatility (see Andersen, Bollerslev, and Diebold, 2010, for a review about volatility jumps). Anderson, Bollerslev, Diebold, and Vega (2003), Andersen, Bollerslev, Diebold, and Vega (2007) nd evidence of signicant in- tradaily price movements in response to macroeconomic announcements. Similarly, Johannes (2004) nds how estimated jumps in a parametric jump diusion model for daily interest rates are associated with specic macroeconomic news events. More in general, volatility models ac- commodate the presence of jumps: Paon and Sheppard (2015) analyse the impact of signed jumps on volatility persistence, while other papers found a limited importance of jumps for fore- casting volatility (Andersen, Bollerslev, and Diebold, 2007, Forsberg and Ghysels, 2007, Busch, Christensen, and Nielsen, 2011). Typically a constant announcement eect is assumed on returns and volatility (see, for ex- ample, Wright, 2012, Haori, Schrimpf, and Sushko, 2016, Joyce, Lasaosa, Stevens, Tong, et al., 2011, Bomm, 2003): we depart from such an approach by allowing for a time varying impact of announcements on volatility. We do so by exploiting the availability of ultra–high frequency based estimators of volatility (see McAleer and Medeiros, 2008, for an exhaustive review), and the possibility of deriving signicant jumps from dierent volatility measures by following Andersen et al. (2007). In this context, one class of models that has proved itself successful in modelling volatility without resorting to logarithms and allowing for a variety of dynamic specications is the Multi- plicative Error Models stemming from the seminal paper by Engle (2002) (see Cipollini and Gallo, 2022, for a recent survey). Cipollini, Gallo, and Otranto (2021) show that MEMs are more robust to the presence of measurement errors in volatility than other volatility models. Caporin, Rossi, and Santucci De Magistris (2017) specify a MEM with jumps (MEM–J) where a latent process is mul- tiplied to the conditional mean of the realized volatility, capturing extremely large realizations lying on the right tail of the distribution, especially in correspondence of nancial crises. More in detail, the multiplicative jump component is generated by a mixture of independent Gamma random variables with the number of components equal to the number of jumps: the laer is a 2 random variable as well, following a Poisson distribution. Here, we propose a new MEM which decouples the dynamics of expected volatility into a component for the continuous part of volatility and one for the discontinuous jump component. It belongs to the class of composite MEM (Brownlees, Cipollini, and Gallo, 2012, Otranto, 2015), which allows for a straightforward identication and interpretation of the jump component. e series of interest is built as intradaily volatility at thirty minute bins as an aggregation of (squared) returns observed at higher frequency, and special care is devoted to accommodating time–of–day eects. Focusing on a short window around each Fed’s communication, our model allows for the identication of the impact of monetary policy announcements on volatility in the bin following the announcement itself. is has the important feature of zooming in to a particular time of the day, rather than relying on the daily scale which could carry some contamination from other relevant events that may occur within the same announcement day. e main goal of estimated results is thus to provide the basis for a new classication procedure of monetary announcements on the basis on their impact on volatility jumps right aer the time of the announcement. We perform our analysis on several tickers, showing that our classication approach is consistent across tickers and provides useful information for both policy makers and investors about the impact of monetary announcements on the volatility of specic sectors of the market. e proposed approach belongs to the category of model-based clustering (see Maharaj, D’Urso, and Caiado, 2019, Liao, 2005, for a review of clustering methods). Importantly, Aghabozorgi, Shirkhorshidi, and Wah (2015) distinguish between whole, subsequent and time-point clustering “aimed at detecting both expected and unusual paerns through the identication of dynamic changes in time series features”. Our approach belongs to the time-point techniques, not in- volving the classication of the full time series under investigation (see Caiado and Crato, 2007, Otranto, 2008, De Luca and Zuccoloo, 2011, for applications on nancial time series). e paper is structured as follows. Section 2 documents the construction of the dataset, where the volatility estimators are dened, together with the identication of signicant jumps (Section 2.1), discusses the empirical regularities of intradaily volatility (Section 2.2) and the relationship between monetary policy announcements and volatility jumps (Section 2.3). Section 3 introduces the model, with estimation results in Section 3.1. e novel model–based classication approach is presented in Section 4, while Section 4.1 shows the empirical results. Finally, on March 17, 2021 and June 16, 2021, the Fed decided to leave the FFR unchanged while increasing its holding of Treasury and agency mortgage-backed securities, corresponding to jumps of 35 and 20% respectively. We allow for a time-varying announcement eect, with each Central Bank’s communication having its own eect on volatility. is allows us to reconstruct expected jumps and jump surprises, and then classify monetary announcements according to whether we detect a local maximum (minimum) or a simple increment (reduction) of the considered series on announcement days. We apply our approach to seven US tickers focusing on the Fed’s monetary policy announce- ments and we highlight the importance of its policy decisions for the evolution of stock volatility and the dynamics of expected jumps. e results on announcement days classication reveal both commonalities and dierences among dierent sectors of the market, with a high interconnec- tion between the IT and nancial sectors, with the pharmaceutical and the customer discretionary sectors being less close. is has the potential to provide useful information to both policy mak- ers – who might acquire new knowledge about the nancial implications of their policies – and investors – for their investment and risk management decisions. e evaluation of our method, based either on the adjusted Rand-index or on the correspon- dence between expected and observed jumps, reveals a good accuracy of our classication, which has the merit of being immediately applicable when an announcement is released. e model could be further rened by extending the number of lags in the specication to check whether it improves the autocorrelation properties of the residuals or it has an impact 19 on the subsequent classication; moreover, a feedback eect could be accommodated within the dynamics of the continuous part making it depend on the lagged expected jump component. In the specication, in line with the logic of the Composite MEM, the two components enter the model additively, while an interesting comparison would be with a model with multiplicative components.
