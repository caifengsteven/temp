# China Anti-Involution Policy Analysis

## Executive Summary

China's "anti-involution" campaign, launched in July 2025, represents a significant shift in economic policy aimed at combating deflationary price wars and promoting sustainable industry consolidation. This analysis examines the policy's implications for basket construction and investment strategy.

## Policy Background

### Definition of "Involution"
- **Original meaning**: Competitive pressures faced by young Chinese in education and workplace
- **Economic context**: Excessive, unsustainable competition among Chinese firms where more resources are invested without increasing returns
- **Government concern**: Price wars leading to collapsing profitability and accelerating deflation

### Policy Launch Timeline
- **June 2024**: Government began highlighting economic dangers of involution
- **July 2025**: Multi-pronged anti-involution campaign officially launched
- **Current status**: Early implementation with some visible impacts

## Target Sectors

### Primary Sectors Affected
1. **Electric Vehicles (EVs)**
   - Price wars initiated by BYD with sharp price cuts
   - Government oversight on pricing and subsidies
   - Industry consolidation encouraged

2. **Solar Panels**
   - Significant overcapacity issues
   - Polysilicon manufacturers discussing $7B consolidation fund
   - Target: acquire and shut down ~1/3 of production capacity

3. **Lithium Batteries**
   - CATL mine closures as part of overcapacity reduction
   - Battery industry consolidation expected

4. **Steel**
   - Traditional overcapacity sector from SSSR 1.0
   - Continued focus on capacity reduction
   - China Shenhua Energy acquisition activities

5. **Food Delivery**
   - Meituan, JD.com aggressive discounting targeted
   - Agreement to end aggressive discounting and promote "fair competition"

6. **Semiconductors**
   - High-tech manufacturing focus
   - Innovation-driven consolidation

## Policy Mechanisms

### Multi-Pronged Approach
1. **Investment Channeling**: Direct funds to advanced manufacturing
2. **Production Control**: Oversee production in highly competitive industries
3. **Pricing Oversight**: Monitor pricing and subsidies in key sectors
4. **Capacity Phase-out**: Continue eliminating obsolete industrial capacity

### Early Results (as of August 2025)
- **Automotive**: Average price discounts declined in July 2025
- **E-commerce**: Alibaba, Meituan, JD.com agreed to end aggressive discounting
- **Industry Consolidation**: Accelerated M&A activity
- **Coal Mining**: China Shenhua Energy asset acquisition program

## Investment Implications

### Positive Factors
1. **Margin Recovery**: Reduced price competition should improve profitability
2. **Industry Consolidation**: Market leaders gain pricing power
3. **Innovation Focus**: Government support for high-tech advancement
4. **Policy Support**: Coordinated government backing

### Risk Factors
1. **Voluntary Compliance**: Private sector pledges may not hold under market pressure
2. **Local Government Conflicts**: Indebted local governments may prioritize short-term revenue
3. **Innovation Concerns**: Deterring overlapping investments may hinder experimentation
4. **Employment Impact**: Industry consolidation could lead to job losses

## Sector-Specific Analysis

### Electric Vehicles
- **Leaders**: BYD, NIO, XPeng, Li Auto
- **Policy Impact**: Pricing discipline, subsidy rationalization
- **Investment Thesis**: Market leaders benefit from reduced price competition

### Solar Energy
- **Leaders**: LONGi Green Energy, JinkoSolar, Trina Solar
- **Policy Impact**: Capacity consolidation, industry restructuring
- **Investment Thesis**: Survivors gain market share and pricing power

### Semiconductors
- **Leaders**: SMIC, Hua Hong Semiconductor, Will Semiconductor
- **Policy Impact**: Innovation support, strategic development
- **Investment Thesis**: Government backing for technological advancement

### Steel
- **Leaders**: Baowu Steel, Ansteel Group, Shagang Group
- **Policy Impact**: Continued capacity reduction, efficiency improvements
- **Investment Thesis**: Consolidation benefits for major players

## Comparison with Global Trends

### Similar Policies Globally
- **Japan**: Industrial policy coordination in 1970s-80s
- **South Korea**: Chaebol system and industrial consolidation
- **Germany**: Mittelstand support and industry coordination

### Unique Aspects of China's Approach
- **Scale**: Unprecedented scope across multiple industries
- **Speed**: Rapid implementation and visible results
- **Coordination**: Central government, local authorities, and private sector alignment

## Implications for Basket Construction

### Sector Selection Criteria
1. **Direct Policy Beneficiaries**: Sectors explicitly targeted by anti-involution measures
2. **Market Leaders**: Companies with pricing power and consolidation advantages
3. **Innovation Focus**: Firms aligned with high-tech manufacturing priorities
4. **Policy Sustainability**: Sectors with long-term government support

### Weighting Considerations
1. **Policy Impact Magnitude**: Higher weights for sectors with stronger policy support
2. **Market Concentration**: Favor sectors with clear market leaders
3. **Profitability Recovery Potential**: Weight based on margin improvement prospects
4. **Innovation Alignment**: Consider government innovation priorities

## Conclusion

The anti-involution policy represents a fundamental shift toward sustainable competition and industry consolidation in China. This creates significant opportunities for market leaders in targeted sectors while posing risks for smaller, less efficient players. The policy's success will depend on maintaining coordination between government objectives and market forces.
