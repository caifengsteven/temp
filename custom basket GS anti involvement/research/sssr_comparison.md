# SSSR 1.0 vs Current Anti-Involution: Comparative Analysis

## Executive Summary

This analysis compares China's Supply-Side Structural Reform (SSSR) 1.0 (2016-2018) with the current anti-involution policy (2025+), highlighting key differences in approach, target sectors, and investment implications for basket construction.

## Historical Context: SSSR 1.0 (2016-2018)

### Background and Motivation
- **Trigger**: Global Financial Crisis response led to massive overcapacity
- **Problem**: CNY 4 trillion stimulus (2008) created excess capacity in traditional industries
- **Symptoms**: Falling prices, declining capacity utilization, zombie enterprises
- **Corporate Debt**: Rose from manageable levels to 160%+ of GDP by 2016

### Policy Objectives (Five Pillars)
1. **Cutting excess industrial capacity** - Primary focus on coal and steel
2. **Reducing leverage in corporate sector** - Address 50+ percentage point increase in debt/GDP
3. **De-stocking property inventories** - Oversupply in lower-tier cities
4. **Lowering costs for businesses** - Tax cuts, fee reductions, red tape elimination
5. **Addressing weak links** - Environmental protection, poverty alleviation, innovation

### Target Sectors and Approach
- **Primary Sectors**: Coal, steel, cement, glass, electricity generation
- **Methodology**: Capacity reduction targets (250 Mta coal, 100-150 Mta steel)
- **Mechanism**: SOE-dominated, government-mandated closures
- **Environmental**: "26+2" winter production cuts for air quality
- **Results**: Significant capacity reduction, price recovery, profit improvement

### Policy Environment
- **Monetary Policy**: Accommodative, supporting deleveraging
- **Fiscal Policy**: Infrastructure investment, shantytown redevelopment
- **Industrial Policy**: SOE consolidation, zombie firm elimination
- **Innovation**: "Made in China 2025" launched concurrently

## Current Anti-Involution Policy (2025+)

### Background and Motivation
- **Trigger**: Deflationary price wars across multiple sectors
- **Problem**: Overcapacity shifted from traditional to high-tech industries
- **Symptoms**: Collapsing margins, accelerating deflation, excessive competition
- **New Challenge**: Private sector dominance requires different approach

### Policy Objectives
1. **Combat deflationary price wars** - Prevent destructive competition
2. **Promote industry consolidation** - Encourage voluntary coordination
3. **Channel investment to advanced manufacturing** - Innovation focus
4. **Maintain pricing discipline** - Sustainable profitability

### Target Sectors and Approach
- **Primary Sectors**: EVs, semiconductors, solar, lithium batteries, food delivery
- **Methodology**: Voluntary agreements, industry coordination
- **Mechanism**: Both POE and SOE participation, market-driven consolidation
- **Innovation**: High-tech manufacturing, advanced materials, AI
- **Results**: Early signs of pricing discipline, consolidation acceleration

### Policy Environment
- **Monetary Policy**: Moderate easing, targeted support
- **Fiscal Policy**: Innovation-focused investment, R&D incentives
- **Industrial Policy**: Public-private coordination, voluntary compliance
- **Innovation**: Advanced manufacturing, digital economy, green technology

## Comparative Analysis

### Similarities
| Aspect | SSSR 1.0 | Anti-Involution |
|--------|----------|-----------------|
| **Core Problem** | Overcapacity | Overcapacity |
| **Government Role** | Active intervention | Active coordination |
| **Consolidation** | Encouraged | Encouraged |
| **Innovation Focus** | Present | Central |
| **Environmental** | Important | Continued |

### Key Differences

#### 1. Sector Focus
**SSSR 1.0**: Traditional heavy industries (coal, steel, cement)
**Anti-Involution**: High-tech and services (EVs, semiconductors, delivery)

#### 2. Ownership Structure
**SSSR 1.0**: SOE-dominated sectors, direct government control
**Anti-Involution**: Mixed POE/SOE, requires voluntary coordination

#### 3. Policy Mechanism
**SSSR 1.0**: Mandatory capacity cuts, administrative closures
**Anti-Involution**: Voluntary agreements, market-driven consolidation

#### 4. Economic Environment
**SSSR 1.0**: Post-crisis recovery, significant monetary easing
**Anti-Involution**: Mature economy, moderate policy support

#### 5. Innovation Emphasis
**SSSR 1.0**: Secondary consideration, traditional upgrade
**Anti-Involution**: Primary driver, advanced technology focus

#### 6. Global Context
**SSSR 1.0**: Domestic focus, limited international implications
**Anti-Involution**: Global supply chain implications, trade considerations

## Investment Implications

### SSSR 1.0 Investment Characteristics
- **Beneficiaries**: Large SOEs in traditional sectors
- **Mechanism**: Government-guaranteed consolidation
- **Risk Profile**: Lower execution risk, policy certainty
- **Returns**: Significant margin recovery, pricing power restoration
- **Duration**: Clear timeline (2016-2020)

### Anti-Involution Investment Characteristics
- **Beneficiaries**: Market leaders in high-tech sectors (both POE and SOE)
- **Mechanism**: Market-driven consolidation with policy support
- **Risk Profile**: Higher execution risk, voluntary compliance
- **Returns**: Innovation premium, sustainable profitability
- **Duration**: Ongoing, longer-term structural shift

## Basket Construction Implications

### SSSR 1.0 Basket Characteristics
- **Sector Allocation**: Heavy weighting in traditional industries
- **Stock Selection**: Focus on largest SOEs
- **Risk Management**: Government policy execution risk
- **Performance Drivers**: Capacity reduction, margin recovery

### Anti-Involution Basket Characteristics
- **Sector Allocation**: Balanced across high-tech and traditional sectors
- **Stock Selection**: Market leaders regardless of ownership structure
- **Risk Management**: Market adoption and voluntary compliance risk
- **Performance Drivers**: Innovation, consolidation, pricing discipline

## Risk Assessment

### SSSR 1.0 Risks (Historical)
- **Execution Risk**: Low (government control)
- **Market Risk**: Moderate (commodity cycles)
- **Policy Risk**: Low (clear mandates)
- **Innovation Risk**: High (traditional sectors)

### Anti-Involution Risks (Current)
- **Execution Risk**: Moderate (voluntary compliance)
- **Market Risk**: High (technology cycles)
- **Policy Risk**: Moderate (evolving framework)
- **Innovation Risk**: Low (innovation-focused)

## Conclusion

The evolution from SSSR 1.0 to anti-involution represents a fundamental shift in China's industrial policy approach. While both address overcapacity, the current policy operates in a more complex environment with higher-tech sectors, mixed ownership structures, and greater reliance on market mechanisms. This evolution requires a more sophisticated basket construction approach that balances traditional consolidation benefits with innovation premiums.

### Key Takeaways for Basket Design
1. **Broader Sector Coverage**: Include both traditional and high-tech sectors
2. **Ownership Agnostic**: Focus on market leaders regardless of POE/SOE status
3. **Innovation Premium**: Weight toward sectors with strong innovation potential
4. **Risk Management**: Account for higher execution uncertainty
5. **Long-term Perspective**: Structural transformation rather than cyclical recovery
